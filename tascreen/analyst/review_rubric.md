# How an analysis is graded (the review agents, and later the reviewer step)

An analysis is: the chart (PNG), the Telegram message (telegram.html: headline, sections
with lights, two scenarios), and the facts it was written from (the .json: every number
the engine computed, and `drawings`). Grade what an ordinary reader receives, but check
it against the facts. Each criterion 1-10 (10 = a professional analyst would sign it).

1. **Accuracy.** Every statement matches the facts and what the chart shows: levels,
   directions, dates, the pattern and its status. A wrong or misleading reading is the
   worst fault. The scenarios use the chart's own levels (up.trigger, down.trigger).
2. **Priorities.** The first line tells the picture now (where the price stands and what
   matters most); the most important level or signal is not buried.
3. **Clarity.** Someone who only knows "support" and "resistance" understands it in half
   a minute: short sentences, a term explained once, no jargon soup, no ambiguity about
   which level is meant.
4. **Professional reading.** Correct technical-analysis logic: zones as areas, a
   breakout needs a close, a divergence is late, overbought is a state not a signal,
   scenarios conditional; nothing overclaimed, nothing important missed (e.g. a fresh
   breakout, a price inside a zone, a failed pattern, a near 52-week high or low).
5. **Usefulness.** A trader knows what to watch next: the level that would start each
   scenario, the next level, and what cancels it, without it becoming advice.
6. **Concision.** No filler, no repetition between sections, nothing that says nothing.
7. **Lights.** Each light (green / red / yellow) fits its section's text and facts.
8. **The chart.** Readable at phone size; the drawn levels are the ones the text names,
   with the same numbers; labels do not collide or hide candles; nothing misleading
   (a level drawn that the text contradicts, a clipped or wrong scale); Hebrew right.

For every problem, name the stock, the section (or "chart"), what is wrong, and a
concrete fix: a rule for the writer, a knowledge note, an engine fact that is missing
or wrong, or a chart change. Prefer fixes that help every stock over one-off rewrites.
